Getting merchants live on the AI Agent, and keeping them live

Memo · Mike Rourke · Head of Product take-home, Zaapi · 28 September 2026

1 The problem

Of every 100 merchants who start setting up the AI Agent, 61 get it live, a median of 19 days after signup. Nine switch it off again, most within 72 hours, so 52 are still live after a month. The agent itself is capable: with complete knowledge it answered 10 of 10 test messages correctly in English, Thai and Bahasa. But setup produces weak agents for most merchants, and hides it from them.

2 What's going wrong

Setup asks a small business to write its own AI agent from scratch, gives it no reliable way to check the result, and then only lets it switch the agent on for everything. In effect, merchants do alone what Zaapi's team does by hand in the $1,900 AI Success Kit. Each of the six merchant quotes in the brief traces to one of four causes.

1No clear path in

You land in the inbox, and onboarding doesn't mention the AI Agent. A pre-connected widget makes merchants look connected. Setup is tabs with no order or progress. → 4 days before any setup step; 21 of 100 never add knowledge. Jakarta: “sitting there since November.”

2Write it all, can't see it

Policies often aren't written down, and nothing suggests scenarios. A 12-page Thai PDF showed “Completed” with half of it read. Jargon and mixed-language screens add to it. → Only 38 of 100 write a scenario (66 test). Manila: “Nobody has written them down.” Chiang Mai: “I'm not sure whether that matters.”

3No real way to test

Test mode is one typed message at a time, with no coverage view. Answers vary run to run, and the built-in check once passed a stale shipping rate as “accurate”. → Bangkok: “How do I know it's ready?” Kuala Lumpur: live Friday, off Saturday.

4All or nothing, no orders

Both go-live routes open Flow Builder, a Pro feature. No way to limit topics, approve replies first or cap spend. The agent can't see orders, so it says “I'm checking” and the customer waits. → 19-day median; 72-hour switch-offs. Ho Chi Minh City: “it'll pass them to the team. Which is us.”

Agent quality and launch decisions didn't line up. The best agent (89) never launched because its owner couldn't see it was good; the weakest (53) went live because nothing warned her. Every agent was safe, but made mistakes merchants wouldn't spot.

4 What I'd change

The main change: Zaapi drafts the agent from what the merchant already has, and shows them whether it's ready before any customer sees it. Everything else supports that.

Two results would change this order. If switch-offs line up with credits running out, pricing and the trial come first. If Kit customers stay live far more often than self-serve ones, drafting comes forward. The order matters more than the dates, which assume the current team.

What I'd deliberately not do

  • Rebuild Flow Builder, or add an AI flow designer, first. Both go-live routes open Flow Builder (Deploy page), which Basic doesn't have (pricing page). A go-live screen outside it works on every plan.
  • Add a percentage slider. Aisha wants a small start, but a random 10% still sends the agent the risky questions. Staging by channel, hours and topic keeps them out. Of six competitors, only Intercom (voice) and Zendesk (A/B tests) split by percentage (competitor review).
  • Make scenarios compulsory. The brief links scenarios to staying live, but careful merchants may just do both. Linh's scenario fired 3 of 3 times and resolved none without order data (persona tests).
  • Build cancel and refund actions first. They're Advanced-only (pricing page), and each marketplace API has its own rules (platform docs). Read-only status answers the most common question sooner.

What would tell me I was wrong

  • Merchants who accept a draft don't go live any faster. Then writing isn't the blocker.
  • The readiness score doesn't predict who stays live. Then replaying old chats isn't a good enough test.
  • Merchants who start small switch off as often as those who launch everything. Then risk isn't the reason.
  • Switch-offs line up with free messages running out. Then it's about price, and the trial and credits need fixing first.
  • Kit sales drop. Then the free version gives too much away, and the topic cap needs tightening.
  • Sale-period sign-ups still don't activate. Then timing matters more than setup, and marketing needs to start earlier.
